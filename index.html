    Smart Structures

    The Saints' own code for structures, written by us and run on the game's chain, free for any pilot. It keeps you and your own tribe safe and lets you set the turret to war, peace or hold. Turrets first; storage and gates later.

    Your turrets

    How to use it

    1. Anchor a turret and bring it online.
    2. Above, press Connect EVE Vault, then Install on the turret, and sign. It starts in peace. It costs a fraction of a test SUI: get some free with Faucet test SUI in EVE Vault.
    3. In the game, open the turret's Edit Assembly, set its Custom dApp link to https://www.thesaintsforge.com/turret and save.
    4. Press F on the turret. Our panel opens beside the game's settings: pick War, Peace or Hold, and add pilots and tribes to the lists. Your wallet asks you to sign each change.
    WarShoots every outsider in range, peaceful visitors and miners included.
    PeaceShoots outsiders who open fire on anything near the base (a structure, a tribe mate, another ship or an NPC), and the enemies on your lists.
    HoldShoots no one.
    Allied tribesSpared until they open fire on anything near the base, an NPC included.
    Enemy tribesAlways shot.
    Ally pilotsNever shot, whatever they do.
    Enemy pilotsAlways shot, even from your own tribe.
    • To add a pilot: select their name in chat, right-click, Copy selected with formatting, and paste it into the panel's Add box. For a tribe, type its tribe id (The Saints are 98000655).
    • You and your own tribe are never shot, unless you put someone on Enemy pilots.
    • NPCs are shot only while they attack: no ammo spent on passers-by.
    • Attackers of the base are shot first, then listed enemies, then the most damaged.
    • Careful with visitors. The game tells a turret only that a ship started shooting, never at what. So in Peace an outsider who fights ferals near your base is shot, even if he is helping (tested on 17 October: an outsider shooting a tribe mate, and one shooting an NPC, were both shot). Put friendly pilots on Ally pilots, or switch to Hold while visitors are around.
    • Mining near a turret has not been tested yet: until it is, treat outside miners as visitors.
    • What the panel saves goes into the turret's description, which anyone can read.

    The Saints Frontier Companion

    A small Windows app that runs beside the game. It reads the game's own logs and what is on your screen, and shows it all on one page in your browser. It never touches the game: no keys, no clicks, no game files changed.

    ⬇ Download for Windows
    1. Right-click the zip › Properties › tick Unblock › OK.
    2. Unzip it and double-click Install. A Saints Frontier Companion shortcut lands on your Desktop and in the Start menu.
    3. Start it. A small window shows each step until your page opens in the browser.
    4. Paste your companion key (below) into the page once.
    Windows 10 or 11 · free · updates itself at every start · release notes

      Beginner's guide · I of 10

      The Crèche is one-way

      Mine it before you leave. Once you warp out, there is no going back.

      • Once you warp out the Crèche is gone; the only way back is warping to a fleet member still inside.
      • Mine its Ingot / Flint rocks first. Layers: Crustal Anorthosite Crystals → Massive Ataxite Matrix core.
      • Take at least 50x Massive Ataxite Matrix for your Refuge. Riders wake up in different systems, and in some of them it is hard to find outside the Crèche.
      • Overloaded = no warp, no Leap. Get under max cargo before you go.
      • Most structures cannot be built near the Crèche.

      Beginner's guide · II of 10

      Your starter ship

      It mines, refines and prints the basics on its own. Leave a fight at about 1,000 health remaining.

      • 2,100 HP hull · 6x Cargo Container (216 m³) · 4x Fuel Bay (2,000 fuel) · 2x Capacitor (200 GJ).
      • One Cutting Laser on a Weapon Receiver, a Gravity Sensor with a Directional Scanner, a Docking Clamp, a Leap, a Thrust Overdrive, an Emergency Printer and a Material Processor.
      • Leave a fight at about 1,000 health remaining.
      • Load it in the Fitting Tool to see every module and try changes before you build them.

      Beginner's guide · III of 10

      Fuel

      Unstable Fuel is free at a Dustline Waystation. Switch off what burns fuel before you log off.

      • Free Unstable Fuel at the Fueling Docks of a Dustline Waystation site: a powered Docking Clamp, within 80 m. Not every system has one.
      • Unstable Fuel is the weakest grade: EU-40 Fuel goes about 3x as far on the same tank.
      • D1 Fuel: 208x Water Ice → 75x D1 Fuel, or 21x Hydrocarbon Residue → 75x D1 Fuel. EU-40 Fuel: 10x Eupraxite → 600x EU-40 Fuel (Eupraxite comes from rift crude).
      • Burn depends on the fuel, not the engine: EU-40 Fuel goes 2.35x as far as D1 Fuel. The R10 engine is fine for it.
      • Before logoff: dock and switch off every module that shows a MW reading in the fitting window (most show 0.1 MW, the Transponder 1.0 MW): those burn fuel. Leave the Power Generator and the Capacitors on: they cost nothing, and a Capacitor switched off recharges from empty when it comes back, which burns far more fuel than it saved.
      • Big systems cost more fuel. Fill up before a trip; there may be no fuel on the route.

      Beginner's guide · IV of 10

      A safe spot and your first home

      A spot nobody can warp to. Build your Refuge and Field Storage there, no Network Node needed.

      • You can only warp to known things: planets, sites, gates, fleet members and your own structures ([H]). A spot nobody can warp to is a safe spot.
      • Two pilots: fleet up. Pilot A warps between two planets that are far apart; while A is mid-warp, pilot B warps to A and lands in deep space. B builds right there: it shows under [H], B's private warp point.
      • A catapult: it drops you at a random spot in the other system, so it is safe by default. Jump a catapult to the next system and build there, or take it back and build where it drops you in your own system.
      • Refuge = 50x Massive Ataxite Matrix. Your respawn point; docked ships cannot be attacked while it stands.
      • Field Storage = 50x Crustal Anorthosite Crystals (30,000 m³).
      • Not next to a dock: Fueling Docks and every other dock have a no-build zone around them (the Refuge excepted).
      • Storage contents are public on the blockchain. Locations are hidden, so never press "Publish Location" on stuff with loot.

      Beginner's guide · V of 10

      Mining and rocks

      Rocks are layered: keep cutting the same spot. The rock family tells you what is inside.

      • Keep cutting the same spot; the layers change as you go in. F2 hides the asteroid so you can grab chunks stuck inside. Scoop range 2,500 m.
      • Crustal Anorthosite Crystals (→ Hydrocarbon Residue → Carbon Weave, Thermal Composites): most of Rind, Glaze, Marrow; under the crust of Bone, Flint, Spall, Char.
      • Massive Ataxite Matrix (→ Palladium): cores of Ingot, Spar, Slag; less under Flint & Spall crust.
      • Hydrated Gossan Matrix (→ Water Ice): rich under Murk's crust; less in Grist, Scale, Spar; traces deep in Mire cores.
      • Primitive Rime Shards (→ Tholin Aggregates + Water Ice): deep in Pitch; outside of Ember, Soot, Glint, Dewdrop; under Mire & Char crust.
      • Primordial Anthraxolite Sludge (→ Kerogen Tar, best source): Ember cores; under the crust of Scale & Char; also Mire & Pitch.
      • Irradiated Anthraxolite Veins (→ Kerogen Tar + Aromatic Carbon Weave): richest under Soot; Glint cores; big veins in Pitch.
      • Irradiated Cryoconite Nodules (→ Aromatic Carbon Weave): Soot cores; Pitch, Char, Ember, Mire.
      • Native Reef Nodules: Ingot.
      • Salvage is minable too: wrecks and debris fields give Debris (→ Salvaged Materials).
      • Mined rocks come back in the same system, and quickly: a stripped field is worth a second visit.

      Beginner's guide · VI of 10

      Making things

      Three machines make everything. Let the Building Calculator work out the recipes, the runs and the ore.

      • Material Processor (on your ship) and the Refinery (a structure, better) turn ore and salvage into materials.
      • Emergency Printer (on your ship) prints the basic parts one at a time, and more than you'd think: the Hull Repairer, the Cutting Laser, mining lenses, Pyro Round and Slug Round, D1 Fuel and EU-40 Fuel. The Mini Printer (a structure) adds Building Foam, Cargo Containers, Capacitors, Fuel Bays, guns and engines, and prints the basic parts in fast batches. It prints D2 Fuel, but not D1 Fuel or EU-40 Fuel.
      • Don't learn the recipes by heart. Open the Building Calculator, say what you want to build, tick the machines you have, and it lists what to mine, refine and print, in order, with the least ore.
      • Lenses, not ammo, for lasers: the Recycled Mining Lens and the Synthetic Mining Lens are the everyday ones; the Needle Lens works but is expensive. Guns take Pyro Round or Slug Round.
      • A Transponder lets you see and use a tribe's catapults and gates. It costs Palladium, which comes from refining Massive Ataxite Matrix; debris fields sometimes drop it loose.

      Beginner's guide · VII of 10

      Building your base, and moving on

      Network Node first, then Refinery, Mini Printer, Relay; the rest in any order. Parts are bulky: plan the trips.

      • 1. Network Node = 8x Printed Circuits + 8x Carbon Weave + 8x Thermal Composites. Build it at your safe spot on an unclaimed anchor; parts must be in your ship cargo. Fuel per hour: 12x D1 Fuel or 3x EU-40 Fuel (Unstable Fuel is rejected). Node offline = nothing prints, and you can't take items out. Nodes show on scans (high EM); dismantling gives the parts back, so move it if it's exposed.
      • 2. Refinery = 15x Reinforced Alloys + 15x Thermal Composites (Build Mode): the structure is better than the ship module.
      • 3. Mini Printer = 15x Printed Circuits + 15x Reinforced Alloys: Building Foam, Cargo Containers, Capacitors, Fuel Bays, guns, engines. 10x Building Foam = 65x Reinforced Alloys + 65x Carbon Weave + 65x Thermal Composites.
      • 4. Relay = 6x Printed Circuits + 6x Carbon Weave + 6x Thermal Composites: a trading post, for later. Sells the Lens 60X for 1,000 EVE, buys Feral Data for 25,000 LUX (100 LUX = 1 EVE).
      • 5. The rest, in any order. Storage: Field Storage = 50x Crustal Anorthosite Crystals (anywhere) · Mini Storage = 250x Crustal Anorthosite Crystals + 250x Massive Ataxite Matrix (at a Node) · Storage = 20x Building Foam. The Heavy Refinery runs the same recipes as the Refinery, faster on most ores. The Printer and Heavy Printer have their own recipe lists: the Heavy Printer is the only maker of some ammo and packaged items. Turrets and Field Sentries defend the base.
      • Moving to a new system: parts are bulky (Carbon Weave 15 m³, Reinforced Alloys & Thermal Composites 10 m³, Printed Circuits 4 m³, Building Foam 47 m³ each). A kit is bigger than it looks: Network Node 232 m³, Refinery 300 m³, Mini Printer 210 m³, and the starter ship holds 216 m³. Fit extra Cargo Containers first (+36 m³ each) or split a kit over two trips. Order: 50x Massive Ataxite Matrix (Refuge first: you respawn there), then the Node with D1 Fuel for it, then the Refinery, then the Mini Printer. Nickel-Iron Veins, Silicon Dust and Water Ice are light (0.1 m³ each): Printed Circuits are worth printing on site.
      • Gates and catapults: public gates run only on crude fuel, 50x EU-40 Fuel or 50x SOF-40 Fuel per jump; player-built gates and catapults draw power from their Network Node and need a Transponder online to show on your map. Read the popup before jumping: "Destination gate unfueled… jump anyway?" means no way back until someone fuels the far side. Never tick "don't ask again".

      Beginner's guide · VIII of 10

      Fitting your ship

      A few modules can never come off, a few have hard limits, and one module keeps you alive.

      • Command Pod, Power Generator, one Capacitor and one Cargo Container can be moved but never removed. Most modules draw 0.1 MW of 15.
      • Hard limits per ship: one Command Pod, one Power Generator, one engine (R10, R25 or R50), one Leap, one Autohelm, one Attitude Controller, and no more than two Weapon Receivers. The game refuses more.
      • Hull Repairer: ~25 HP/s, your best survival module. Structural Brace: +375 HP.
      • Capacitor / Blackstart Cell: +100 GJ and +1.2 GJ/s each, until the Power Generator's spare power caps the recharge: 15 MW minus what your modules draw (about 13 GJ/s on most fits), so past 10 or 11 Capacitors the extra ones add storage, not recharge. Fuel Bay +500 fuel (10 cells) · Fuel Blister +250 fuel (5 cells): same fuel per cell. Cargo Container: 36 m³ each.
      • Two Weapon Receivers = two lasers. Mining nonstop with both needs about 9x Capacitor + 1x Blackstart Cell; check your own fit in the Fitting Tool.
      • The Directional Scanner needs a Gravity Sensor. The Gravity Sensor scan (M1) names rocks 300+ km away.
      • Try all of it in the Fitting Tool: it shows hold, fuel hours, whether the capacitor holds up, and power before you build.

      Beginner's guide · IX of 10

      Staying alive

      Shoot back at NPCs right away, leave at 1,000 health, and never let the capacitor run dry.

      • Shoot back at NPCs right away: waiting is how new players die. Gankers camp docks and spawning rocks; don't idle there.
      • Players: check before you return fire. A glitch makes a nearby miner's laser look like it is hitting you when it isn't. Return fire only once you see actual damage on your screen.
      • Warping needs capacitor charge. Run it empty and you can't warp out. 4x Capacitor + 1x Blackstart Cell: one laser + repairer lasts ~7 min of nonstop fighting (about 4 with the Transponder on). 8x + 1x: one laser + repairer never runs dry.
      • Weapons: Cutting Laser 12 km, ramps 2.3x over 16 s, resets on a target switch, mines too · Stuttergun 35 km, full damage from shot one, can't mine · Needle 25 km laser, ramps 2.5x over 20 s, mines worse.
      • Starter ferals: Ostler hits hardest → Luthier → Caird. A pack does ~14 HP/s: a repairer holds. Keep range on fast orbiters (Ostler, Luthier): up close your laser misses almost every shot.
      • Grave ferals (threat 4 systems): the full pack averages ~21 HP/s with bursts over 50, so one repairer barely holds. Kill the Grave Wright first or leave.
      • Player bases defend themselves. Turrets lock only 2 ships (bring 4+), their shields take 2 days to recharge, a Mini Turret hits for about 400, up to 600. Turrets ramp their fire rate up on a ship that has just arrived instead of opening with a full volley; attack the base and they fire at full rate. Field Sentries: 4,800 HP, ~100 DPS each within 25 km, several can kill a full ship in 18 seconds; out-range them with a Stuttergun.
      • Fighting a base: fight from behind cover (behind a building, a Mini Turret missed 91 of 99 shots), lasers work on structures (215–260 per shot), and never go back alone to finish one.

      Beginner's guide · X of 10

      Scanning and loot

      Fly to 60 km of a marker, scan with the narrowest cone. Empty container = someone got there first.

      • Strength = range; range grows with the square root of strength. Resolution = blur; 1 mrad = 1 km off per 1,000 km away.
      • Gravity Sensor markers are fuzzy, about 8% of the distance: that's why a marker moves or vanishes as you fly in. The Directional Scanner is ~27x sharper. Sensors add range and, by the client's numbers, sharpen passive markers; the Directional Scanner's sharpness is fixed. The Ion Sensor is the EM channel: without it, EM range drops to a third.
      • Use the smallest cone: narrow 10 GJ, wide 50 GJ, omni 90 GJ, and a smaller cone blooms less EM signature, so others are less likely to find you.
      • Containers are faint (10 t, EM 25; a ship is 10,000 t, EM ~300). Fly to ~60 km of the marker, then scan with a narrow cone. Nothing? Move 20 km closer and scan again.
      • Empty container = looted, not a dud. Containers never despawn and they do refill, but not often: most at downtime (which isn't every day), a few during the day. Found one empty? Check it again after the next downtime.
      • Which sites: most loot at Ossuary Mill (also lots of Debris), Derelict Quarry, Respite Anchorage, Fringe Latticeway. No ferals placed: Dustline Waystation. Graven Works: the client lists drone spawn points there, but the game decides whether they actually spawn. Graven Works and the Ossuary Mill ruins carry salvage to cut. Most guarded: Longwatch Redoubt. Natural sites (Stone/Ancient Cluster, Shale, Grove, Garden, Crossing) are rocks only.
