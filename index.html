    Smart Structures

    The Saints' own code for structures, written by us and run on the game's chain, free for any pilot. It keeps you and your own tribe safe and lets you set the turret to war, peace or hold. Turrets first; storage and gates later.

    Your turrets

    How to use it

    1. Anchor a turret and bring it online.
    2. Above, press Connect EVE Vault, then Install on the turret, and sign. It starts in peace. It costs a fraction of a test SUI: get some free with Faucet test SUI in EVE Vault.
    3. In the game, open the turret's Edit Assembly, set its Custom dApp link to https://www.thesaintsforge.com/turret and save.
    4. Press F on the turret. Our panel opens beside the game's settings: pick War, Peace or Hold, and add pilots and tribes to the lists. Your wallet asks you to sign each change.
    WarShoots every outsider in range, peaceful visitors and miners included.
    PeaceShoots outsiders who open fire on anything near the base (a structure, a tribe mate, another ship or an NPC), and the enemies on your lists.
    HoldShoots no one.
    Allied tribesSpared until they open fire on anything near the base, an NPC included.
    Enemy tribesAlways shot.
    Ally pilotsNever shot, whatever they do.
    Enemy pilotsAlways shot, even from your own tribe.
    • To add a pilot: select their name in chat, right-click, Copy selected with formatting, and paste it into the panel's Add box. For a tribe, type its tribe id (The Saints are 98000655).
    • You and your own tribe are never shot, unless you put someone on Enemy pilots.
    • NPCs are shot only while they attack: no ammo spent on passers-by.
    • Attackers of the base are shot first, then listed enemies, then the most damaged.
    • Careful with visitors. The game tells a turret only that a ship started shooting, never at what. So in Peace an outsider who fights ferals near your base is shot, even if he is helping (tested on 17 October: an outsider shooting a tribe mate, and one shooting an NPC, were both shot). Put friendly pilots on Ally pilots, or switch to Hold while visitors are around.
    • Mining near a turret has not been tested yet: until it is, treat outside miners as visitors.
    • What the panel saves goes into the turret's description, which anyone can read.

      Beginner's guide · I of 10

      The Crèche is one-way

      Mine it before you leave. Once you warp out, there is no going back.

      • Once you warp out the Crèche is gone; the only way back is warping to a fleet member still inside.
      • Mine its Ingot / Flint rocks first. Layers: Crustal Anorthosite Crystals → Massive Ataxite Matrix core.
      • Take at least 50x Massive Ataxite Matrix for your Refuge. Riders wake up in different systems, and in some of them it is hard to find outside the Crèche.
      • Overloaded = no warp, no Leap. Get under max cargo before you go.
      • Most structures cannot be built near the Crèche.

      It mines, refines and prints the basics on its own. Leave a fight at about 1,000 health remaining.

      • Load it in the Fitting Tool to see every module and try changes before you build them.

      Beginner's guide · III of 10

      Fuel

      Unstable Fuel is free at a Dustline Waystation. Switch off what burns fuel before you log off.

      • Free Unstable Fuel at the Fueling Docks of a Dustline Waystation site: a powered Docking Clamp, within 80 m. Not every system has one.
      • Unstable Fuel is the weakest grade: EU-40 Fuel goes about 3x as far on the same tank.
      • D1 Fuel: 208x Water Ice → 75x D1 Fuel, or 21x Hydrocarbon Residue → 75x D1 Fuel. EU-40 Fuel: 10x Eupraxite → 600x EU-40 Fuel (Eupraxite comes from rift crude).
      • EU-40 Fuel goes 2.35x as far as D1 Fuel on the same tank. Which engine burns which fuel is not settled yet: check before you fill up with a grade you have not used in that engine.
      • Before logoff: dock and switch off every module that shows a MW reading in the fitting window (most show 0.1 MW, the Transponder 1.0 MW): those burn fuel. Leave the Power Generator and the Capacitors on: they cost nothing, and a Capacitor switched off recharges from empty when it comes back, which burns far more fuel than it saved.
      • Big systems cost more fuel. Fill up before a trip; there may be no fuel on the route.

      Beginner's guide · IV of 10

      A safe spot and your first home

      A spot nobody can warp to. Build your Refuge and Field Storage there, no Network Node needed.

      • You can only warp to known things: planets, sites, gates, fleet members and your own structures ([H]). A spot nobody can warp to is a safe spot.
      • Two pilots: fleet up. Pilot A warps between two planets that are far apart; while A is mid-warp, pilot B warps to A and lands in deep space. B builds right there: it shows under [H], B's private warp point.
      • A catapult: it drops you at a random spot in the other system, so it is safe by default. Jump a catapult to the next system and build there, or take it back and build where it drops you in your own system.
      • Refuge = 50x Massive Ataxite Matrix. Your respawn point; docked ships cannot be attacked while it stands.
      • Field Storage = 50x Crustal Anorthosite Crystals (30,000 m³).
      • Not next to a dock: Fueling Docks and every other dock have a no-build zone around them (the Refuge excepted).
      • Storage contents are public on the blockchain. Locations are hidden, so never press "Publish Location" on stuff with loot.

      Beginner's guide · V of 10

      Mining and rocks

      Rocks are layered: keep cutting the same spot. The rock family tells you what is inside.

      • Keep cutting the same spot; the layers change as you go in. F2 hides the asteroid so you can grab chunks stuck inside. Scoop range 2,500 m.
      • Crustal Anorthosite Crystals (→ Hydrocarbon Residue → Carbon Weave, Thermal Composites): most of Rind, Glaze, Marrow; under the crust of Bone, Flint, Spall, Char.
      • Massive Ataxite Matrix (→ Palladium): cores of Ingot, Spar, Slag; less under Flint & Spall crust.
      • Hydrated Gossan Matrix (→ Water Ice): rich under Murk's crust; less in Grist, Scale, Spar; traces deep in Mire cores.
      • Primitive Rime Shards (→ Tholin Aggregates + Water Ice): deep in Pitch; under Mire & Char crust; the game's rock table also puts it outside of Soot, Glint, Dewdrop (and Ember, but our Ember cuts held none).
      • Primordial Anthraxolite Sludge (→ Kerogen Tar, best source): Ember cores; under the crust of Scale & Char; also Mire & Pitch.
      • Irradiated Anthraxolite Veins (→ Kerogen Tar + Aromatic Carbon Weave): richest under Soot; Glint cores; big veins in Pitch; smaller ones in Grist, Ember, Mire.
      • Irradiated Cryoconite Nodules (→ Aromatic Carbon Weave): Soot cores; Pitch, Char, Ember, Mire.
      • Native Reef Nodules: Ingot.
      • Salvage is minable too: wrecks and debris fields give Debris (→ Salvaged Materials).
      • Mined rocks come back in the same system, and quickly: a stripped field is worth a second visit.

      Beginner's guide · VI of 10

      Making things

      Three machines make everything. Let the Building Calculator work out the recipes, the runs and the ore.

      • Material Processor (on your ship) and the Refinery (a structure, better) turn ore and salvage into materials.
      • Emergency Printer (on your ship) prints the basic parts one at a time, and more than you'd think: the Hull Repairer, the Cutting Laser, mining lenses, Pyro Round and Slug Round, D1 Fuel and EU-40 Fuel. The Mini Printer (a structure) adds Building Foam, Cargo Containers, Capacitors, Fuel Bays, guns and engines, and prints the basic parts in fast batches. It prints D2 Fuel, but not D1 Fuel or EU-40 Fuel.
      • Don't learn the recipes by heart. Open the Building Calculator, say what you want to build, tick the machines you have, and it lists what to mine, refine and print, in order, with the least ore.
      • Lenses, not ammo, for lasers: the Recycled Mining Lens and the Synthetic Mining Lens are the everyday ones; the Needle Lens works but is expensive. Guns take Pyro Round or Slug Round.
      • A Transponder lets you see and use a tribe's catapults and gates. It costs Palladium, which comes from refining Massive Ataxite Matrix; debris fields sometimes drop it loose.

      Beginner's guide · VII of 10

      Building your base, and moving on

      Network Node first, then Refinery, Mini Printer, Relay; the rest in any order. Parts are bulky: plan the trips.

      • 1. Network Node = 8x Printed Circuits + 8x Carbon Weave + 8x Thermal Composites. Build it at your safe spot on an unclaimed anchor; parts must be in your ship cargo. Fuel per hour: 12x D1 Fuel, 8x D2 Fuel or 3x EU-40 Fuel (Unstable Fuel is rejected). Node offline = nothing prints, and you can't take items out. Nodes show on scans (high EM); dismantling gives the parts back, so move it if it's exposed.
      • 2. Refinery = 15x Reinforced Alloys + 15x Thermal Composites (Build Mode): the structure is better than the ship module.
      • 3. Mini Printer = 15x Printed Circuits + 15x Reinforced Alloys: Building Foam, Cargo Containers, Capacitors, Fuel Bays, guns, engines. 10x Building Foam = 65x Reinforced Alloys + 65x Carbon Weave + 65x Thermal Composites.
      • 4. Relay = 6x Printed Circuits + 6x Carbon Weave + 6x Thermal Composites: a trading post, for later. Sells the Lens 60X for 1,000 EVE, buys Feral Data for 25,000 LUX (100 LUX = 1 EVE).
      • 5. The rest, in any order. Storage: Field Storage = 50x Crustal Anorthosite Crystals (anywhere) · Mini Storage = 250x Crustal Anorthosite Crystals + 250x Massive Ataxite Matrix (at a Node) · Storage = 20x Building Foam. The Heavy Refinery runs the same recipes as the Refinery, faster on most ores. The Printer and Heavy Printer have their own recipe lists: the Heavy Printer is the only maker of some ammo and packaged items. Turrets and Field Sentries defend the base.
      • Moving to a new system: parts are bulky (Carbon Weave 15 m³, Reinforced Alloys & Thermal Composites 10 m³, Printed Circuits 4 m³, Building Foam 47 m³ each). A kit is bigger than it looks: Network Node 232 m³, Refinery 300 m³, Mini Printer 210 m³, and the starter ship holds 216 m³. Fit extra Cargo Containers first (+36 m³ each) or split a kit over two trips. Order: 50x Massive Ataxite Matrix (Refuge first: you respawn there), then the Node with D1 Fuel for it, then the Refinery, then the Mini Printer. Nickel-Iron Veins, Silicon Dust and Water Ice are light (0.1 m³ each): Printed Circuits are worth printing on site.
      • Gates and catapults: public gates run only on crude fuel, 50x EU-40 Fuel or 50x SOF-40 Fuel per jump (22x EU-90 Fuel or 25x SOF-80 Fuel); each end of a gate has its own tank, and fuel put in cannot be taken out; player-built gates and catapults draw power from their Network Node and need a Transponder online to show on your map. Read the popup before jumping: "Destination gate unfueled… jump anyway?" means no way back until someone fuels the far side. Never tick "don't ask again".

      Beginner's guide · VIII of 10

      Fitting your ship

      A few modules can never come off, a few have hard limits, and one module keeps you alive.

      • Command Pod, Power Generator, one Capacitor and one Cargo Container can be moved but never removed. Most modules draw 0.1 MW of 15.
      • Hard limits per ship: one Command Pod, one Power Generator, one engine (R10, R25 or R50), one Leap, one Autohelm, one Attitude Controller, and no more than two Weapon Receivers. The game refuses more.
      • Hull Repairer: ~25 HP/s, your best survival module. Structural Brace: +375 HP.
      • Capacitor / Blackstart Cell: +100 GJ and +1.2 GJ/s each, until the Power Generator's spare power caps the recharge: 15 MW minus what your modules draw (about 13 GJ/s on most fits), so past 10 or 11 Capacitors the extra ones add storage, not recharge. Fuel Bay +500 fuel (10 cells) · Fuel Blister +250 fuel (5 cells): same fuel per cell. Cargo Container: 36 m³ each.
      • Two Weapon Receivers = two lasers. Mining nonstop with both needs about 9x Capacitor + 1x Blackstart Cell; check your own fit in the Fitting Tool.
      • The Directional Scanner needs a Gravity Sensor. The Gravity Sensor scan (M1) names rocks 300+ km away.
      • Try all of it in the Fitting Tool: it shows hold, fuel hours, whether the capacitor holds up, and power before you build.

      Beginner's guide · IX of 10

      Staying alive

      Shoot back at NPCs right away, leave at 1,000 health, and never let the capacitor run dry.

      • Shoot back at NPCs right away: waiting is how new players die. Gankers camp docks and spawning rocks; don't idle there.
      • Players: check before you return fire. A glitch makes a nearby miner's laser look like it is hitting you when it isn't. Return fire only once you see actual damage on your screen.
      • Warping needs capacitor charge. Run it empty and you can't warp out. 4x Capacitor + 1x Blackstart Cell: one laser + repairer lasts ~7 min of nonstop fighting (about 4 with the Transponder on). 8x + 1x: one laser + repairer never runs dry.
      • Weapons: Cutting Laser 12 km, ramps 2.3x over 16 s, resets on a target switch, mines too · Stuttergun 35 km, full damage from shot one, can't mine · Needle 25 km laser, ramps 2.5x over 20 s, mines worse.
      • Starter ferals: Ostler hits hardest → Luthier → Caird. A pack does ~14 HP/s: a repairer holds. Keep range on fast orbiters (Ostler, Luthier): up close your laser misses almost every shot.
      • Grave ferals (threat 4 systems): the full pack averages ~21 HP/s with bursts over 50, so one repairer barely holds. Kill the Grave Wright first or leave.
      • Player bases defend themselves. Turrets lock only 2 ships (bring 4+), their shields take 2 days to recharge, a Mini Turret hits for about 400, up to 600. Turrets ramp their fire rate up on a ship that has just arrived instead of opening with a full volley; attack the base and they fire at full rate. Field Sentries: 4,800 HP, ~100 DPS each within 25 km, several can kill a full ship in 18 seconds; out-range them with a Stuttergun.
      • Fighting a base: fight from behind cover (behind a building, a Mini Turret missed 91 of 99 shots), lasers work on structures (215–260 per shot), and never go back alone to finish one.

      Beginner's guide · X of 10

      Scanning and loot

      Fly to 60 km of a marker, scan with the narrowest cone. Empty container = someone got there first.

      • Strength = range; range grows with the square root of strength. Resolution = blur; 1 mrad = 1 km off per 1,000 km away.
      • Gravity Sensor markers are fuzzy, about 8% of the distance: that's why a marker moves or vanishes as you fly in. The Directional Scanner is ~27x sharper. Sensors add range and, by the client's numbers, sharpen passive markers; the Directional Scanner's sharpness is fixed. The Ion Sensor is the EM channel: without it, EM range drops to a third.
      • Use the smallest cone: narrow 10 GJ, wide 50 GJ, omni 90 GJ, and a smaller cone blooms less EM signature, so others are less likely to find you.
      • Containers are faint (10 t, EM 25; a ship is 10,000 t, EM ~300). Fly to ~60 km of the marker, then scan with a narrow cone. Nothing? Move 20 km closer and scan again.
      • Empty container = looted, not a dud. Containers never despawn and they do refill, but not often: most at downtime (which isn't every day), a few during the day. Found one empty? Check it again after the next downtime.
      • Which sites: the client gives the most loot spawn points to Ossuary Mill (also lots of Debris), Graven Works, Derelict Quarry, Respite Anchorage and Fringe Latticeway; what actually sits there on a given day is the game's call. No feral spawn points in its layout: Dustline Waystation. Graven Works: the client lists drone spawn points there, but the game decides whether they actually spawn. Graven Works and the Ossuary Mill ruins carry salvage to cut. Most guard spawn points: Longwatch Redoubt (our two visits found it empty, its containers locked). Natural sites (Stone/Ancient Cluster, Shale, Grove, Garden, Crossing) have no NPC spawn points in their layouts, but that doesn't make them safe: Saints have met NPCs in an Outer Shale.

      Lore

      The Frontier's hidden story in plain words: the KEEP broadcasts, the people who came before, the machines they left behind. Every line says how sure we are.

      ✠ Courtesy of ✠Straelynand the lore hunters of The Saints · last updated 24 October 2026

      What Straelyn's group has pieced together about EVE Frontier's lore, written to be read, not decoded. Every claim says how sure we are. The evidence (ids, measurements, symbol-by-symbol work) is kept in Straelyn's research notes. Last updated 24 October 2026.

      How sure are we?

      • ✅ Known: seen in game, or read straight from the game's own files.
      • 🟡 Likely: fits several checks, but leans on something we couldn't check ourselves.
      • 💭 Guess: our interpretation. Interesting, not proven.

      The short version

      1. ✅ The game sends cryptic videos through the KEEP Monitor: short orange "broadcasts", and "slices" that players find and upload.
      2. ✅ The videos use their own alphabet. One word in it, EIMUR, matches a real system name, so we know five of its letters for certain.
      3. 🟡 With the community's alphabet, the first slice reads as a sentence, roughly: "Hear this deity's desire: Exclave, to stop my creator's anger, stop worshipping this envy."
      4. 🟡 One broadcast seems to name the Xeroti Tidofiza. ✅ That's a real but unseen 30 km monster in the game files, related to the Moon Eaters. 🟡 Veritas, who played earlier cycles, remembers "Xeroti" as the Moon Eater.
      5. ✅ The people who lived here before are gone. What's left: statues of people carved into asteroids in our home system, ruins, corpses, and machines still doing their old jobs with nobody to give them orders.
      6. ✅ CCP has ruins ready at the old civilisations' capitals (including our home, G48), but they're switched off for now.
      7. ✅ We are Riders: minds brought "out of stasis" by KEEP into worn synthetic bodies (see section 8).
      8. ✅ The files hide white dwarfs, neutron stars and six black holes; the black-hole sites are an "endgame destination", switched off.

      1. The KEEP broadcasts

      ✅ In game there's a window called the KEEP Monitor. Its Archive has two kinds of video:

      • Broadcasts: 39 short clips of orange, pixelated footage (ships, towers, star maps), each ending on a diamond-grid logo. They're numbered in batches that seem to follow the game's cycles. 💭 Players call the sender the Keeper.
      • Slices: only two so far, VKS and VN0, each "uploaded by" a player character.

      ✅ Slices are real items. The game files list 40 of them. Ten are "shattered" pieces that can be combined at an industry facility into two bigger "Stacks", and the game files them all under "Video Fragments". So whenever someone finds a slice and uploads it, a new video appears in the archive. 38 slices haven't been found yet.

      💭 Where do slices drop? The game files don't say. Our best lead is the Conservator drones (below), which hunt for exactly this kind of old data and steal it from containers.

      2. The alphabet

      The videos are written in a blocky stencil font. Here it is with what we've worked out:

      • ✅ E, I, M, U, R are certain. They spell EIMUR, which shows up in a broadcast and, crisp and clean, on a star map screen in a story video. Eimur is a real system, and it hosts the game's Eimur event site.

        🟡 Eimur has moved. The files point at two old systems (30010445 from the game's labels, 30023342 from the Cycle 6 map). Straelyn reports that 30023342 is the old Eimur and that the game now marks a different system as Eimur. For now we're assuming that's 30010445, which Scav's digging found. Nobody has seen its id in game.

      • 🟡 The other letters are likely. They come from an alphabet the player community worked out. We checked it the only way we could: it lines up perfectly with the VKS slice's title, letter slot by letter slot, and it agrees with EIMUR everywhere they overlap.
      • 🟡 Four more letters from the two map labels ("ROTI TIDOFI" and "H.SJD.V01"): D is the likeliest (the same glyph sits in the D slot of both labels); F, J and S are guesses from their slots.
      • Not seen yet: Q, W, Z and most numbers.
      • A few odd rules: a long "Ō" is written as two dotted squares, and the glitch effect in the videos hides parts of letters but never adds any.

      3. What the first slice says

      🟡 The VKS slice's title, letter by letter:

      TŌ PEY ILARA / YE VO GHŌ / YE RI HATO
      EXCLAVE, KŌNI GE KŌHA, … TŌ PEY ILARA

      🟡 The community translates it as: "Hear this deity's desire: Exclave, to stop my creator's anger, stop worshipping this envy." We can confirm the letters but not the translation: nobody we know has checked the language.

      ✅ "Exclave" is a real word in the game's text (an "Exclave Technocore", "Exclave Sentry", "Exclave Keep" data).

      💭 Our guess: the "deity" and its "creator" are connected to the Xeroti and Moon Eaters, since the broadcasts point there (next section).

      4. Moon Eaters and the Xeroti

      Left: the Mooneater design. Right: the Xeroti Tidofiza. Both are the game's own renders.

      • ✅ The machines came first. The game describes "Moon Eater-class" factories, like the Aestasium Forge: "a testament to human ambition now left to its own devices… its creators likely long gone." Its job was to grind moons and asteroids into a synthetic "intelligent mineral".
      • ✅ Then they went wild. "Feral" Mooneaters, called Sovereigns, about 3 km across: "binding these Sovereigns to long dead intentions… a living monument to the permanence of greed." Veterans remember them roaming and destroying bases in earlier cycles.
      • ✅ Bigger things exist in the files, never seen. A drone-built Moon Eater about 100 km across, "built by the effort of thousands of rogue drones", effectively impossible to kill. And the Xeroti Tidofiza, about 30 km across: "a corrupted bulge craving substance… rehearsing the frenzied logic of its eventual freedom", with "Symbiote" drone escorts.
      • 🟡 Read with the community alphabet, a broadcast's caption says "…ROTI TIDOFI…", part of the name Xeroti Tidofiza, over a picture of a huge structure. That a made-up-looking reading lands on a real monster's name is a good sign the alphabet is right.
      • ✅ The Mooneater debris field (a dense field of broken rock) is in every Outer Blue Drift belt, but without the Mooneater. Our group has been through plenty and never seen one.
      • 🟡 A veteran's memory. Veritas, a trusted player from The Saints who played earlier cycles, remembers "Xeroti" as the Moon Eater: the Xeroti and the Moon Eaters may be one thing under two names. We couldn't check this in the files or in game ourselves; it rests on Veritas's word.
      • ✅ The Xeroti broadcast points at a real system. The one that reads "ROTI TIDOFI" also shows the map label H.SJD.V01, which is system 30017471 (named I1J-SCL in the Cycle 6 map). It has only ordinary belts in the game files, and nobody has visited. ✅ It appears in two broadcasts, E6C4-1G and E6C4-1H, which carry the same two labels (we checked the video files).
      • 💭 Our reading: the Moon Eaters began as human mining machines, went feral, and the rogue drones built their own giant version. The Xeroti may be what grows on or out of them. If they return, it'll probably be as a big server event, not a site you stumble on.

      5. The people who came before

      • ✅ Statues of people are carved into five asteroids in the Graven Works belt of our home system G48. They only exist in the four starter systems. "Graven" means carved.

        Close-ups (UI hidden, taken 24 October 2026): rows of robed figures standing in carved niches, a relief of a figure beside tall columns, a row of figures beside a huge wheel, giant faces, and an inside-out hall of colossal figures.

        A hauler drifts near one of the statue rocks, in a Graven Works in a Venture capital. ✅ The hauler model is shared by several things in the game, so we can't say which one this is:

      • ✅ The game names three old civilisations: Synod, Venture and Clan. Each has "capital" systems. G48 is a Venture capital, the only starter system that's a capital at all.
      • ✅ Ruins are ready but switched off. Every Venture capital (G48 included) has a "Venture Enclave Ruins" site waiting, almost every Clan capital a "Clan Trace", and four Synod capitals have "Capital Ruins". CCP hasn't turned them on yet.
      • ✅ Everywhere else, people survive only as remains: mummified corpses, a "Cairn" described as a monument to the dead, Synod outposts, abandoned haulers.
      • 💭 The statues are probably Venture monuments: people honoured by the miners who worked that quarry.

      6. Machines with no one left to obey

      The Watcher guard tower (the game's own render).

      • ✅ Conservators (drones called Caird, Luthier, Ostler, Wright, Shambler, Dowser, Scrivener) are live. The game says going feral "only bolstered their desire to protect the work of their ancestors."
        • Two of them, the Dowser and Scrivener, raid containers and leave a calling card called a Feral Trace:

        "a remnant of curiosity left behind after its owner snatched the objects of its desire." If you find one in your storage, you've been robbed.

        • Their Watcher towers stand guard at their own structures in inner belts, with Conservators around them. (An

        earlier version of this page said they guard the fuel depots. They don't.)

      • ✅ Trader drones exist in the files, neutral: "A remnant of an ancient logistics network, long since vanished… carrying and exchanging goods according to protocols that…" (the game's text breaks off there).
        • 🟡 Veritas remembers them in earlier cycles: roaming traders that would buy things from players' relays.

        That's his memory, not something we could check in the files or in game.

        • 💭 Nobody in our group has met one this cycle; the Conservators treat them as enemies, so they may not last long.
      • ✅ Daemons (not seen yet): Production, Arsenal and Sentry Daemons, machine minds that "dream of fleets and colossi". Each has an "Exorcised" version: "Sorrow replaces ambition… It is merely a delay."
      • ✅ The old feral hives (Osa, Okryda, Termit, Tsikada, Sarana) from earlier cycles survive as empty husks in some belts.

      7. Ruined gates

      • ✅ The game files hold a hidden network of 114 very long stargate links (up to 7,000 light years), separate from the normal gates, and a site called "Ruined Gate Network". It isn't active. Nobody has jumped one.
      • 💭 When it switches on, there'll probably be a ruined gate to find and repair at each end.

      8. Who woke us up: the Keeper

      • ✅ When you start a cycle, the game shows a Keeper Note: "You are placed in a temporary clone body, provided by Keep free of charge. The Awakening Protocol has been activated, your mind has been transferred out of stasis… Awake, eternally." So we were asleep, stored somewhere, and KEEP woke us. The whole effort is called Project Awakening.
      • ✅ We're called Riders. Our bodies are worn-out synthetic shells that connect to something called Retia (Latin for "nets"). The game never explains Retia.
      • ✅ The note mentions a Bridgehead Key to break through a quarantined stargate. Three such keys exist in the files (08, 86 and 37), with no description and no way to make them.

      9. Strange matter

      • ✅ Crude Matter (from rifts) is described as "a sea of emotion" whose colour changes with "whichever passion dominates".
      • ✅ Defiled Crude Matter is the same stuff scarred by "human greed": it "enters this universe broken". Nobody we know has found any. The Xeroti (the terrifying giant) is coded to want it.
      • ✅ Salvage is heavy. Debris, Crystalline Refuse and Industrial Waste are 6 to 200 times denser than ore, which is why a hold of it slows you to a crawl.

      10. Loot with a story

      • ✅ Old Synod haulers drift in some belts. Near the statues in G48, one had a docking point and floating boxes that held Drone Signal Records and Miner Hiring Forms.
      • ✅ Those belong to a set of 11 curios: an Oil Painting, a Stranger's Head, Synod Propaganda, a Very Strange Musical Instrument and more. For now they're just souvenirs of the people who lived here.

      11. Odd stars and black holes

      • ✅ Some belts only form around unusual stars, so the files quietly reveal white dwarfs (hundreds of systems), neutron stars (33 belts), binary stars, and six black-hole systems, three of them near the centre of the galaxy.
      • ✅ The black-hole sites are marked as an "endgame destination" and are switched off.
      • ✅ The game doesn't tell us which star each system has. We can only confirm what we've seen: QN8 has 3 white dwarfs and a red dwarf.
      • ✅ Near U.MZ: system 30020522 (132 LY) has the same tell-tale belts as QN8. The nearest neutron star is 899 LY away; the nearest black hole is 2,032 LY.

      12. Campfire theories (no proof)

      💭 Are we Jovian? In EVE Online, the Jove were an ancient people who came through the EVE Gate in slow, fuel-hungry ships, built a vast empire, then caught an incurable genetic disease. Some slept in stasis to survive. An early, never-released Frontier ship is called the Jauv. Straelyn's idea: a Jove branch reached the Frontier through wormholes that were later shattered, and got cut off when Caroline's Star hit EVE's wormhole space in 2014. Their minds went into storage (Retia?) to escape the disease, and the Keeper is waking them up.

      💭 Did they meet the Triglavians? The Triglavians live in the Abyss, a realm of triangles and threes. The Frontier is full of triangles, an old hauler talks about "exposure to the Trinary", and the galaxy is centred on three black holes. Maybe the Jove fought the Triglavians here and were cursed for it. Scav goes further: he thinks the Frontier is Abyssal space.

      💭 Emotion-made matter. If Crude Matter is shaped by feelings, CCP might one day tie it to what players do.

      Nothing in Frontier's own text names the Jove, the Triglavians or New Eden. These are stories that fit the clues, not facts.
